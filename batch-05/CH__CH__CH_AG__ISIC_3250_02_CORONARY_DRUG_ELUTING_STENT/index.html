Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum nominal share capital for a Swiss AG is CHF 100,000; at least 20% of the nominal value of every share (i.e. minimum CHF 20,000) must be fully paid up in cash at incorporation and evidenced by a bank certificate confirming deposit on a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto) at a Swiss-regulated bank; funds remain blocked until Commercial Register entry and must be at the free disposal of the board thereafter; any contribution in kind under Art. 628 para 2 CO requires a licensed auditor's formation report.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety approval by the Zug fire prevention authority covering solvent storage, EX-zone electrical classification, and an automatic extinguishing system; structural load and vibration-attenuation inspection of the cleanroom facility by the Zug building inspectorate (Bauinspektorat Kanton Zug); occupational clearance under SUVA including appointment of a laser safety officer, workplace hygiene monitoring for isopropyl alcohol and acetonitrile vapours, and medical surveillance of cleanroom personnel.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum nominal share capital for a Swiss AG is CHF 100,000; at least 20% of the nominal value of every share (i.e. minimum CHF 20,000) must be fully paid up in cash at incorporation and evidenced by a bank certificate confirming deposit on a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto) at a Swiss-regulated bank; funds remain blocked until Commercial Register entry and must be at the free disposal of the board thereafter; any contribution in kind under Art. 628 para 2 CO requires a licensed auditor's formation report.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Swiss Federal Tax Administration (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung, ESTV / Administration fédérale des contributions, AFC / Amministrazione federale delle contribuzioni, AIC) and its mandatory E-VAT portal at www.estv.admin.ch; cantonal tax administrations are separately responsible for cantonal and communal corporate income/capital tax registration in the canton of the registered office (e.g., Zug, Zurich).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity that conducts taxable supplies in Switzerland and exceeds the Swiss VAT registration threshold must file an application with the ESTV/FTA using the official E-VAT electronic portal (ISiTax). If the foreign entity has no domestic seat, it must designate a VAT representative residing or established in Switzerland. The application must identify the governing corporate form (e.g., foreign stock company equivalent to Aktiengesellschaft), intended activity, projected turnover, and effective date of liability. Upon approval, the FTA assigns a nine-digit VAT identification number in the form CHE-XXX.XXX.XXX (RFC/MWST/TVA/IVA). The UID-CH business identification number from the Federal Statistical Office is integrated for tax-administrative purposes. Registration with the cantonal tax administration for corporate income/capital tax is done via the Swiss Central Compensation Office or the cantonal portal; distinct cantonal company tax numbers are issued separately.

Default tax classification

Available election forms

Swiss law provides no US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections include VAT group registration (Steuergruppe), corporate income tax group/fiscal unity under Art. 62 DBG, application of the participation exemption for qualifying dividend and capital-gain income, notional interest deduction on excess equity (notioneller Zinsabzug), R&D super-deduction of 150% in certain cantons, election to allocate costs under the transaction-based method for transfer pricing, and the option for cross-border B2B services to be treated as received at the recipient's place of establishment for VAT purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 100,000 (annual turnover from taxable supplies; for a non-resident foreign entity, CHF 100,000 of taxable supplies made within Switzerland unless the supplies are already exempt or only subject to VAT at a reduced rate under special provisions)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating is available under Art. 23 VATA for the supply of goods that are physically exported from Switzerland by the supplier or on its behalf, including goods delivered into bonded warehouses or customs-free zones, provided the export takes place before first use/enjoyment in Switzerland and the supplier obtains verifiable proof of export. For services, zero rating applies when the place of supply is located abroad (B2B reverse-charge rules), but physically exportable goods in the drug-eluting stent value chain require a Swiss customs export declaration. The stent manufacturer must demonstrate the transport chain from Swiss territory to the foreign destination; subcontract cooling chain or logistics service documentation must be preserved.

  • Swiss Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung / déclaration en douane d'exportation) confirmed by the Federal Office for Customs and Border Security (BAZG/FOCBS)
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract containing export terms, product description, ISIC/HS classification, and foreign buyer reference
  • Transport document (Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR letter of conveyance) proving physical removal of the goods from Switzerland
  • Certificate of arrival / customs-entry evidence issued by the destination-country customs authority where required by FTA audit practice for high-risk medical exports
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 credit advice evidencing payment from the foreign buyer where the FTA or the bank requires corroboration of the export transaction

Switzerland does not operate a real-time government fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control (CTC) regime for domestic VAT. Electronic invoicing (QR-bill, eBill, EDI, PDF/A) is legally recognised and permitted, but the exporter must retain the original data in an unaltered, integrity-protected format for 10 years. There is no API-based mandatory clearance with the FTA for each invoice; the FTA may require that electronic invoices incorporate a verifiable authenticity and integrity mechanism (e.g., advanced electronic signature, hash, or secured EDI transmission). Statutory invoice particulars include supplier name and VAT ID, recipient, date, type and quantity of good, consideration, applicable rate, and VAT amount. Absence of the mandatory particulars for export documentation renders the zero rating invalid.

The FTA will disallow the 0% rate and re-render the export taxable retroactively if the customs declaration is deficient, the transport evidence is incomplete, or the exporter fails to substantiate actual departure from Switzerland. Re-rendering triggers additional VAT at 8.1%, default interest at 4.5% per annum, and administrative fines under Art. 96/97 MWSTG. In cases of intentional tax evasion, the fine may reach CHF 100,000; fraudulent or deliberately misleading zero-rating claims may attract criminal pecuniary penalties and imprisonment up to three years under general criminal tax law. Unjustly retained input VAT is clawed back in full, and the limitation period for reassessment is 10 years in cases of tax evasion; all export and customs records must be retained for this period.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Switzerland has full current and capital account convertibility under the Swiss Federal Constitution and the Federal Act on the Free Movement of Capital; no general exchange control restrictions exist on profit remittance, dividends, or repatriation of capital for Swiss-resident Aktiengesellschaft companies; however, the SNB retains the right to introduce temporary safeguard measures in the event of severe currency distortion, and banks impose enhanced due diligence for high-risk medical device manufacturers. Profit repatriation is subject solely to Swiss withholding tax at 35% on dividends unless reduced by double taxation treaty or Swiss participation relief; no prior regulatory approval is required for outbound remittances if anti-money laundering declarations and tax compliance declarations are in order

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender requirement exists under Swiss law; export receipts may be retained in foreign currency or converted at the exporter's discretion; Swiss banks follow FINMA circular 2011/1 regarding the clarification of the legal framework for the holding of foreign currencies; no mandatory conversion ratio applies, and the Swiss National Bank does not enforce any obligation to exchange export earnings into CHF

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB) and the Swiss State Secretariat for International Finance (SIF) for financial market policy; no separate foreign exchange administration exists, as Switzerland maintains a unitary monetary authority framework

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for primary treasury clearing because Swiss law does not grant full banking licenses to electronic money institutions for commercial lending or complex cross-border Class III MedTech operational accounts; FINMA-regulated fintech licenses (fintech licence under Art. 1b of the Swiss Banking Act) permit deposit-taking up to CHF 100 million on an interest-free basis but do not cover letter of credit issuance, export factoring, or complex multi-currency treasury settlements. Offshore EMIs such as those licensed under the UK FCA or Lithuanian LB may operate only as payment initiation or account information service providers under the Swiss Payment Services Act (PSA) and cannot hold client funds in Switzerland without a Swiss banking license; practical viability is restricted to ancillary pre-funded disbursement wallets, with all main liquidity clearing executed via SWIFT members.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections and shareholder loans must be effected via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 through the Swiss SIC (Swiss Interbank Clearing) system with a clear payment purpose code. For equity capital contributions, remittance should reference purpose code 'P04 – Capital contribution / equity participation' and the bank must receive a copy of the notarized capital increase resolution and the Swiss commercial register deposit confirmation. For shareholder loans, use purpose code 'P03 – Loans between non-financial enterprises' or OECD purpose category '810 – Financial loans' depending on the sending bank's classification; no exchange control approval is required. Banks will verify that the foreign payer is the same entity or beneficial owner as recorded in the Swiss registers, and large cash amounts or payments from unregulated money-service businesses will be rejected. For payments exceeding CHF 100,000, Swiss banks must comply with the DBA (Duty to report suspicious financial activity) under the AMLA and may require a written explanation of the corporate purpose. The LC instruments must include a compliance clause stating the beneficiary bank is SWIFT-connected and that goods are classified under HS code 9021.40 (cardiovascular devices, including coronary stents). Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

8.5% Swiss federal direct corporate income tax (statutory rate computed on after-tax profit; effective pre-tax burden 7.83%); aggregate federal plus cantonal plus communal ordinary corporate income tax rate normally 11.9%–21.0% depending on canton (Zug approx. 11.9%; Zurich approx. 19.7%; Geneva approx. 14.0%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% Swiss withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer, Art. 1 VStG) on every profit distribution, constructive dividend, and on interest-free loans or excessive licence payments to shareholders where recharacterised as distribution of profit; for a Swiss AG distributing a dividend to its PRC parent, 35% is initially withheld and then partially refunded under the DTA refund procedure.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The corporate tax return must be submitted within 6 months after the fiscal year end; for calendar-year companies the cantonal deadline is commonly set between 31 March and 30 June of the following year; extensions up to a further 6 months are available upon written request; non-compliance results in a final mandatory 30-day term (peremptory).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File under the Swiss TP documentation regulation (SR 642.123) once consolidated group turnover exceeds CHF 80 million in a year and the Swiss entity carries out cross-border transactions with related parties; Country-by-Country Report required at consolidated group turnover of CHF 900 million. Below the CHF 80 million threshold, a full TP study is not formally prescribed but is commercially indispensable for a Swiss principal/licence-holder manufacturing Class III stent products, since ESTV audit practice requires a transactional benchmark (typically TNMM) whenever intercompany flows involve IP, procurement or distribution.

Economic substance scope: Switzerland does not apply an explicit Economic Substance Regulations statute as in the UAE/EU, but enforces an equivalent substance doctrine: a Swiss AG must be effectively managed and administered in Switzerland — board meetings in Swiss territory, a Swiss-resident authorised officer, a physical office, and personnel commensurate with the functions performed. The patent box is available only where a substantial part of the qualifying R&D was performed in Switzerland (nexus approach). A letterbox company that merely holds stent IP licences or collects royalties while decision-making occurs in Shanghai or Shenzhen will be denied treaty benefits under the beneficial-ownership rule, may be re-attributed as a permanent establishment of the Chinese parent, and will trigger retrospective reassessments; the historically privileged domicile-company regime was abolished in 2020.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Corporate Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to 100% of the evaded federal tax and equivalent cantonal fine (Art. 175 DBG), default interest at 4%, unlimited reassessment within the 10-year absolute prescription period (20 years for false documents); the fine is not covered by insurance and is personally payable.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: the signatories and directors are prosecuted individually for tax evasion; imprisonment up to 3 years applies in hard cases; the cantonal prosecutor may impose a travel ban, asset freeze, and passport revocation; the AG cannot indemnify its directors for criminal fines.
Withholding Tax Default / Constructive Dividend

Statutory sanction: The Swiss AG as debtor company remains liable for the 35% WHT plus 4% interest under the Swiss Withholding Tax Act (VStG), payable within 30 days of the distribution; the DTA refund for the Chinese parent is forfeited if the Swiss company did not withhold; repeated default leads to coercive fines of up to 10% of the unpaid amount.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly refrain from withholding become joint personal debtors for the withheld amount; seizure of personal assets in Switzerland is available; intentional fraudulent withholding or attempted DTA fraud is a criminal offence under the VStG with imprisonment up to 3 years, irrespective of the corporate veil.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty refunds and retrospective tax assessments with interest; money laundering under Art. 305bis of the Swiss Criminal Code carries imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine up to CHF 5 million (CHF 10 million in severe cases); confiscation of the full proceeds under Arts. 70–73 SCC; Swissmedic withdrawal of the Class III device licence; banks close the CHF account and freeze assets.

Director personal liability: Prima facie evidence of unlawful activity: the corporate veil is entirely pierced; directors, fiduciaries and providing attorneys face pre-trial detention and travel bans; they are liable for unpaid social-insurance debts under Art. 52 AHVV and for civil damages for the full unpaid tax, with a prohibition from acting as directors for up to 10 years.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Upward adjustment of the Swiss taxable profit by the ESTV recharacterising the excess royalty or margin as a hidden dividend; supplementary assessment of 35% WHT and 4% interest on the deemed distribution to the Chinese parent; in the case of intentional inadequate documentation, penalty tax up to 100% of the evaded amount (Art. 175 DBG in conjunction with Art. 58a DBG).

Director personal liability: Directors who sign mispriced intercompany contracts are jointly liable for civil damages to the company for the full tax difference under the Swiss Code of Obligations; criminal liability falls on members of the board and the finance officer; because the directors are domiciled in Switzerland, asset seizures and travel bans are enforceable; individual directors cannot invoke the corporate shield.
