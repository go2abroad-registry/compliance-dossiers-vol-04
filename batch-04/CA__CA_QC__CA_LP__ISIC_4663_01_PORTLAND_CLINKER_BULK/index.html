Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for federal GST/HST (Business Number via My Business Account portal) and Revenu Québec for Québec Sales Tax (QST/TVQ) administered through the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (NEQ) and the clicSÉQUR Affaires portal; Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) administers customs export reporting.

E-government filing portal

1) Nexus and capacity: confirm the LP carries on business in Quebec or elects voluntary registration; for a non-Canadian LP, file the extra-provincial immatriculation declaration with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec to obtain the NEQ and appoint a resident representative (mandataire). 2) Registration: submit Form RC1 with CRA to obtain the federal Business Number and GST account (the LP is a separate 'person' registrant under the Excise Tax Act, s. 272); concurrently file the enterprise registration and QST application with Revenu Québec so that the QST number is linked to the NEQ; elect an effective date aligned with the export supply. 3) Post-registration: enroll for Peppol/EDI issuance, confirm the partnership fiscal period election, and prepare Quebec partnership return (TP-600) and federal allocation forms (T5013) for partner reporting. Non-resident LPs may register voluntarily even below the threshold to zero-rate exports and claim input tax credit/refund entitlements; issuance typically occurs within 5-20 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Canada; a Quebec limited partnership (société en commandite) is fiscally transparent for income tax (partners taxed under ITA s. 96) but is treated as a distinct taxable registrant person for GST/QST purposes. Available elections: partnership fiscal period election (ITA s. 249.1(4)); voluntary non-resident GST/QST registration and associated quarterly/annual filing elections; joint 'related person' election under ETA s. 156 for supplies among registrants (inapplicable to third-party export sales); no elective re-characterization of the LP as opaque is available under Canadian law, and hybrid mismatch rules (ITA ss. 18.4-18.6) apply mechanically to cross-border payments without elective carve-outs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 per annum gross worldwide taxable supply revenue (GST and QST small-supplier threshold); export-only foreign LPs may register voluntarily below this threshold to claim input tax refunds and issue zero-rated invoices.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods supplied for export are zero-rated under Part V of Schedule VI to the Excise Tax Act and the corresponding QST Act provisions where the supplier is a registrant, the goods are sold and physically removed from Canada, and the goods are exported promptly (CRA administrative position: generally within 60 days of supply). Portland clinker delivered free on board to an ocean vessel at a Canadian port for shipment to a foreign buyer qualifies if possession and risk pass in Canada and no use is made in Canada prior to export; associated freight and handling services supplied from a place in Canada to a destination outside Canada are likewise zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed through CBSA (CAED/export reporting system, required for goods exceeding CAD 2,000)
  • Ocean Bill of Lading showing exporter and foreign destination, matching the commercial invoice quantity and Incoterm
  • Commercial invoice with Incoterms (e.g., FOB/FAS/CIF) identifying the Quebec supplier, the overseas buyer, and the export destination
  • Packing list and appointment of the vessel/voyage reference
  • Foreign import entry documentation (e.g., US CBP Form 7501) as corroborative third-party proof of export
  • Payment evidence bundle: SWIFT MT103/MT202, letters of credit settlement/negotiation advices, or bank statements; an Inward Remittance Certificate is not a CRA statutory requirement but is accepted when structured as a corroborating bank document

No real-time fiscal clearance, pre-approval stamping, or continuous transaction control exists in Canada. Quebec has enacted a phased B2B e-invoicing mandate (LQ 2022) slated to come into force progressively for large enterprises, using interoperable structured formats aligned with the Pan-Canadian Peppol network; suppliers exporting goods should nevertheless issue an invoice containing the required federal and Quebec disclosure elements and must retain the electronic invoice metadata to substantiate zero-rating upon audit.

Normal reassessment window is four years from the assessment date (ETA s. 298 and QST equivalent), with no time limit where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is established. A failed export zero-rating reverses the supply into the taxable 14.975% combined GST/QST regime, and the LP must remit the tax with arrears interest. False or misleading statements attract a minimum penalty of the greater of CAD 250 and 25% of the overstated rebate/understated net tax (ETA s. 285), plus equivalent Revenu Québec QST penalties; deliberate evasion may result in criminal prosecution (ETA s. 239). CRA and Revenu Québec will match the invoice, ocean bill of lading, customs declarations, and the actual funding trail; missing or inconsistent documents trigger full clawback of input tax refunds claimed by the LP. Canada imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is a documentary banking and trade-finance convention that preserves the integrity of the export payment trail and mitigates the risk of retrospective recharacterization of a domestic taxable sale.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a free-floating, fully convertible currency and has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations. Capital and current account transactions are convertible without prior ministerial approval, exchange licensing, or quantitative ceilings. Profit remittance hurdles are entirely tax-driven, not FX-control-driven: a Quebec LP is pass-through for Canadian tax purposes, so a non-resident limited partner's allocated Canadian-source income may attract Part XIII withholding tax under the Income Tax Act (Canada) (usually 25% statutory, often reduced to 15% under the Canada-US treaty if applicable), which must be remitted to the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) by the GP before the wire is cleared. Banks will classify clinker trade payments as ordinary current-account trade settlements using SWIFT purpose codes (e.g., 'GDS', 'CLA', 'FTT'), and such classification attracts zero convertibility restriction. Quebec QST/GST compliance and CRA prescribed interest-rate records are ancillary hurdles to be satisfied on audit, but they do not impede wire transfer clearing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Canada imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender, holding-period, or mandatory conversion ratio. Proceeds from ocean-vessel clinker exports or re-exports may be retained in USD or any foreign currency in a designated foreign-currency account at a Canadian Schedule I bank. The Minister of Finance's emergency powers under the Currency Act are dormant and impose no current timeline or ratio. The only binding overlay is the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA): record all EFTs of CAD 10,000 or more, report large-cash transactions of CAD 10,000 or more, perform sanctions screening under the Special Economic Measures Act (SEMA), and retain records for at least five years. Anti-structuring rules forbid splitting receipts to evade FINTRAC reporting; therefore clinker sales invoices should explicitly prohibit payment splitting.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), Department of Finance Canada (emergency exchange-control powers under the Currency Act), and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) as the AML/CFT supervisory authority. Within Quebec, the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) regulates financial-sector conduct, money services businesses, and derivative-related activities, and the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) registers the société en commandite (Limited Partnership).

Feasibility: Low-Medium. Offshore digital EMIs are not viable as the primary treasury rail for a bulk clinker LP. An offshore EMI engaging with Canadian clients must either register as a Money Services Business (MSB) with FINTRAC and maintain a Quebec compliance address acceptable to the AMF, or operate through an exempt intra-group structure; otherwise it cannot lawfully receive CAD from Canadian payors. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound protocol for the LP: foreign investors transmit by SWIFT MT103 with a clear purpose code in field 70, e.g., 'CAPT' or 'CAPI', combined with a memo identifying the Quebec LP registration number and the words 'CONTRIBUTION TO CAPITAL OF LIMITED PARTNERSHIP'. The receiving bank must hold the LP's account number with its SWIFT BIC (e.g., National Bank BNDCCAMMINT, RBC ROYCCAT2, BMO BOFMCAM2), and the remitter's institution must include the full legal name, address, and account numbers. All inbound EFTs equal to or exceeding CAD 10,000 must be reported to FINTRAC. Payments must be routed through Payments Canada's Lynx (high-value settlement) for CAD-denominated transfers, or via Fedwire/CHIPS with the Canadian bank's US correspondent for USD-denominated capital injections. Breaking a single capital contribution into sub-CAD-10,000 tranches to avoid FINTRAC reporting is illegal structuring. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Low-Medium. Offshore digital EMIs are not viable as the primary treasury rail for a bulk clinker LP. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

N/A - fiscally transparent Limited Partnership (societe en commandite) under Quebec Civil Law; no entity-level CIT. Reference combined federal+Quebec corporate headline rate: 26.5% (Federal 15% + Quebec 11.5%). Non-corporate partners face top combined marginal income tax rate of 53.31%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Part XIII statutory rate 25% on distributions taxable as dividends; for non-resident partners of the LP, the allocation of Canadian-source income is generally taxed under Part I (s. 2(3) ITA) at the partner's regular graduated rate if the partner carries on business through the LP. Treaty reduction under Canada-US DTA: 5% if a corporate partner holds 10% or more of the LP; otherwise 15%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation and Form T106 (Information Return for Non-Arm's Length Transactions) are required when the aggregate value of transactions with each non-resident related party exceeds C$1,000,000 for goods, or C$50,000 for services, interest, rents and royalties. Form T1134 is additionally required if the LP has a foreign affiliate. Country-by-Country reporting (Master File and Local File) applies if the ultimate parent's consolidated revenue is C$750 million or more.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not maintain a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulation; however, the GAAR under section 245 of the Income Tax Act (and section 1026 of the Quebec Taxation Act) disables tax benefits from transactions whose primary purpose is avoidance and that lack genuine economic substance. A Quebec LP dealing in bulk Portland clinker must demonstrate actual commercial activity: physical inventory, commercial premises in Quebec, maintained books and records, and real risk assumption through the general partner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Criminal Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Section 239(1) Income Tax Act: fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax plus imprisonment for up to 5 years; Quebec Taxation Act s. 1023: fine of 100% to 200% of the evaded Quebec tax and imprisonment; CRA and Revenu Quebec joint enforcement actions.

Director personal liability: The general partner (and any corporate director of the general partner) is personally liable for unremitted source deductions, GST/QST, and Part XIII withholding under s. 227.1 ITA and s. 325 ETA; limited partners who participate in control of the partnership lose the limited-liability shield and become jointly and severally liable; travel bans may be imposed under the Canada Border Services Agency enforcement procedures.
Shell Company and GAAR Abuse

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under GAAR denying the tax benefit and levying a 25% federal penalty (s. 245(4) ITA) and a 15% Quebec penalty; the CRA may impose a 10% gross-negligence penalty under s. 163(2) on top of reassessments; professional fees cannot be deducted for resolving GAAR adjustments.

Director personal liability: The directors of the general partner may be personally assessed for tax amounts where the general partner corporation has failed to remit; a finding of wilful avoidance exposes directors to director-liability proceedings and potential criminal liability for abetting evasion; the Quebec registraire may dissolve the LP administratively after 2 years of failure to file the mandatory annual declaration, stripping the shield and causing automatic general-partner liability.
AML and Wire-Transfer Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act: administrative monetary penalties up to C$500,000 for summary conviction offenses and up to C$2,000,000 on indictment for entities; individuals (including partners) face up to 5 years imprisonment; the CRA may also impose foreign-asset reporting penalties of C$2,500 (ordinary negligence) to C$12,000 (gross negligence) for unreported foreign property over C$100,000.

Director personal liability: The directors of the general partner, and the general partner itself, are subject to FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties in their own capacity; officers who knowingly failed to implement an AML program are personally exposed to indictment-level prosecution including imprisonment; civil forfeiture can be applied to the LP's assets and bank accounts.
Non-Resident Partner Withholding Failure

Statutory sanction: Section 227(8) ITA: penalty of 10% of the amount withheld at source if in the ordinary course, 20% if the failure was made knowingly or with gross negligence; compounded daily interest on unremitted amounts; the CRA can assess the LP as trustee and hold the entire outstanding withholding tax recoverable from the partnership's bank accounts and sales receivables on the clinker sales.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner corporation are personally liable for the unremitted Part XIII amounts under s. 227.1 ITA without needing fraud or gross negligence, upon initial demand; Quebec similarly permits Revenu Quebec to pursue the general partner's personal assets and to impose a certificate of tax debt that is enforceable as a civil judgment, including seizure of shares and a travel bar pending substantial payment.
