Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Tax Administration (FTA / Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung, ESTV) - VAT Main Division and Federal Direct Tax Division; Swiss Federal Office for Customs and Border Security (BAZG/FOCBS) for export evidence; official e-portal: https://www.estv.admin.ch and VAT e-services „MWST online / TVA-TP“; cantonal tax administrations (e.g. Steueramt Zug) for cantonal and municipal corporate income and capital tax, which receive the registration ex officio via the commercial register data transfer.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of the Swiss Aktiengesellschaft in the Cantonal Commercial Register (Handelsregisteramt), the Federal Statistical Office (BFS/UST) automatically allocates the Swiss Business Identifier „UID“ in the ZEFIX central name index (format CHE-123.456.789, Federal Act on the Swiss Business Identifier, UIDG). The UID with extension „MWST“ constitutes the VAT number. VAT registration is filed with the ESTV through the „Anmeldung zur Mehrwertsteuerpflicht / Umsatzmeldung“ e-form (Art. 66-67 LTVA/MWSTG). Non-established foreign enterprises must additionally designate a Swiss fiscal representative domiciled in Switzerland, who is jointly and severally liable for the VAT obligations (Art. 67 para. 3 LTVA). The Cantonal Tax Office registers the AG for federal, cantonal and municipal corporate income, and capital taxes by way of commercial-register notification; separate notifications are due for the OASI/AVS compensation fund and for Swiss withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) on dividends at the statutory 35% rate, subject to treaty reduction or reclaim.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: the Swiss AG is mandatorily a corporate taxpayer at all three levels - federal direct tax at 8.5% of net profit (effective approximately 7.83% after the tax-deduction rule) and cantonal/municipal profit and capital taxes at combined effective rates of approximately 11% in Zug to approximately 20% in higher-tax cantons. Resident companies are taxable on worldwide income, with the exemption method applied to foreign permanent establishments and foreign immovable property (Art. 52 DBG / Art. 20 StHG). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the Swiss AG is mandatorily a corporate taxpayer at all three levels - federal direct tax at 8.5% of net profit (effective approximately 7.83% after the tax-deduction rule) and cantonal/municipal profit and capital taxes at combined effective rates of approximately 11% in Zug to approximately 20% in higher-tax cantons. Resident companies are taxable on worldwide income, with the exemption method applied to foreign permanent establishments and foreign immovable property (Art. 52 DBG / Art. 20 StHG). The AG has no shareholder pass-through; profits are taxed at entity level and dividend distributions are subject to the Swiss 35% withholding tax unless the participation exemption or a treaty applies.

Available election forms

Swiss law provides no check-the-box or partnership-transparency election for a stock company; the legal-form classification is mandatory. Available elections and reliefs: (i) VAT group taxation (Mehrwertsteuer-Gruppenbesteuerung, Art. 12 LTVA) for closely-merged Swiss entities consolidating into one taxpayer; (ii) flat-rate input VAT deduction under the Saldosteuersatz procedure for eligible SMEs with turnover below CHF 5,005,000; (iii) election between the calendar year and a commercial fiscal year, subject to tax authority approval; (iv) participation relief (Qualified Participation Deduction) under Art. 70 DBG and Art. 28 StHG for dividend and capital-gain income meeting the 10% shareholding and CHF 1,000,000 thresholds; (v) partial deemed tax credit (Teilbesteuerungsverfahren) for qualifying participation dividends effective 1 January 2025; (vi) transitional status-company reliefs (holding, domiciliary, mixed) following the TRAF 2020 corporate tax reform; (vii) hybrid-mismatch limitation rules derived from the Swiss DBA-Paket and ATAD without any entity-election mechanism. No fiscal unity or group taxation exists for Swiss corporate income tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8.1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CHF 250,000 global annual taxable turnover for resident enterprises (increased from CHF 100,000 effective 1 January 2025, Art. 10 para. 2 LTVA). Non-resident enterprises must register when Swiss-source service turnover exceeds CHF 100,000 per year or when distance/goods supplies into Switzerland exceed CHF 250,000 per year. Voluntary registration without threshold is permitted; registration may be waived below the threshold unless input VAT refund claims are sought.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of acetate eyeglass frames qualify for the 0% VAT rate with full deduction of input VAT (echte Steuerbefreiung, Art. 23 para. 2 ch. 1 LTVA) where the goods physically leave Swiss territory for a foreign customer. The exporter must hold the electronic Swiss customs export declaration issued by BAZG and, for goods destined to EU business customers under the reporting procedure (Meldeverfahren, Art. 54 LTVA), a signed Lieferbescheinigung (delivery certificate) obtained from the importing customer. Failing physical export, the supply is domestic and taxable at the standard 8.1% rate, including goods placed into a Swiss consignment or fulfillment warehouse. Services supplied to foreign wholesale customers are outside Swiss VAT under the B2B place-of-supply rule (Art. 8 LTVA). Switzerland maintains no foreign-exchange controls and imposes no statutory export-proceeds repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark below reflects the schema default and standard Swiss banking and trade-finance practice for outward remittance collection evidenced by SWIFT MT103 statements.

  • Electronic Swiss customs export declaration (BAZG e-dec/Passar) including the export approval number and the Ausfuhrschein confirmation
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) / Air Waybill / CMR rail-road consignment note evidencing physical movement across the Swiss border
  • Commercial invoice (DE/EN/FR) and packing list cross-referenced to the customs declaration
  • Lieferbescheinigung (delivery certificate, Art. 54 LTVA) for EU-bound goods under the reporting procedure or the foreign customs import declaration (e.g. EU SAD/ATLAS import document)
  • Bank SWIFT MT103 / inward remittance certificate evidencing foreign payment collection
  • Transport insurance certificate and warehouse release documents supporting the complete export chain

No real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory tax-authority API, and no digital stamping regime applies in Switzerland. The ESTV accepts structured electronic invoices (Peppol BIS 3.0, EIC, eBill, EDI) for VAT audit purposes; invoices issued to the federal administration (B2G) must be delivered electronically through the Peppol/eBill channel from 1 January 2026 under the Swiss Confederation e-invoicing strategy. All VAT-relevant records, including export evidence, must be produced electronically on audit and retained for 10 years (Art. 81 LTVA).

The ESTV verifies export zero-rating through document-based audits of the BAZG export records and Lieferbescheinigungen. Missing or contradictory export proof triggers retroactive reassessment of VAT at the 8.1% standard rate plus statutory default interest published by the Federal Department of Finance under Art. 87 LTVA (approximately 4-5% p.a.) and the clawback of input VAT previously deducted on the export supply. Fraudulent zero-rating claims constitute VAT evasion (Art. 97 LTVA), with fines up to four times the evaded tax in willful cases and up to one times in negligent cases; procedural violations (Art. 96 LTVA) attract administrative fines up to CHF 8,000; criminal tax fraud (Steuerbetrug) using forged documents is punishable by imprisonment of up to three years and monetary penalties (Art. 100 LTVA / Art. 186 DBG). The right to assess expires after 5 years, extended to 10 years where evasion is established (Art. 42 LTVA); export records must be kept for 10 years (Art. 81 LTVA).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full capital and current account convertibility. Switzerland imposes no exchange-control approvals on inbound equity injections, shareholder loans, trade payments, profit remittances, or liquidation proceeds. Dividends paid by a Swiss AG are subject to the federal withholding tax (Verrechnungssteuer) of 35%, reduced or refundable under an applicable double taxation treaty; interest on shareholder loans is generally not subject to Swiss withholding tax if at arm's length. No minimum local-currency holding period or repatriation approval applies to the eyewear distribution business.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Switzerland has no statutory obligation to surrender, convert, or repatriate export proceeds within any prescribed timeline. Receivables from the wholesale export of ophthalmic eyewear frames may remain in foreign currency in domestic or foreign accounts. Only ex-post statistical reporting to the SNB under its statistical data-collection ordinance (SR 932.111) may apply once foreign assets or liabilities of the Swiss resident enterprise exceed the statutory thresholds, and this is informational, not a convertibility or custody control.

Central bank supervision: Swiss National Bank (SNB); monetary authority and FX statistics supervisor. No separate foreign-exchange-control administration exists. Banking and anti-money-laundering supervision is exercised by the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA). The Swiss International Financial Services Standard (SIF) framework and the CDB Ordinance govern client due diligence in onboarding.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with strict legal constraints. FINMA-licensed fintech entities holding a banking licence under Art. 1b Banking Act may accept up to CHF 100 million in client deposits without paying interest, making them compliant for low-balance operating flows but not for capital escrow or material trade settlement. Offshore EMIs licensed in the EU or UK are not permitted to conduct deposit-taking or payment services for Swiss residents in a cross-border manner without a Swiss banking or FinSA licensing gateway; they are viable only as front-end payment facilitators for Platform_Pay collections, with a nightly sweep to a FINMA-regulated Tier-1 account.

Offshore EMIs licensed in the EU or UK are not permitted to conduct deposit-taking or payment services for Swiss residents in a cross-border manner without a Swiss banking or FinSA licensing gateway; they are viable only as front-end payment facilitators for Platform_Pay collections, with a nightly sweep to a FINMA-regulated Tier-1 account.
