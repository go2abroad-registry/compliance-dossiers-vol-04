Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) – Federal corporate/income tax and Goods and Services Tax/Harmonized Sales Tax (GST/HST); portal: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html. In Quebec: Revenu Québec – Quebec Sales Tax (QST) and provincial income taxes; portal: https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/home/.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign entity participating in a Quebec Limited Partnership (société en commandite): (1) Register the LP with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec (REQ) to obtain a Quebec Enterprise Number (NEQ); (2) apply to the CRA for a federal Business Number (BN) and, if the partnership has taxable supply activity in Canada, a GST/HST registration account; (3) register with Revenu Québec for QST where the threshold (CAD 30,000 of taxable supplies) is exceeded; (4) each non-resident partner must obtain a Canadian tax identifier (BN for non-resident corporate partners or a Social Insurance Number for individuals) and must file a Canadian income tax return or the appropriate s.216 election for Canadian-source income. Non-resident partners may also be subject to s.116 compliance for dispositions of taxable Canadian property.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. For Canadian federal and Quebec provincial income tax purposes, a Quebec LP is fiscally transparent; the partners are subject to tax on their respective shares of income, and the partnership itself is not an income taxpayer. The LP must nevertheless register for GST/HST/QST as a person for indirect tax purposes.

Available election forms

No Canadian equivalent to the U.S. check-the-box election exists. An LP may adopt a non-calendar taxation year only with CRA approval. For GST/HST, a partnership may make designated-member elections to centralize filings. Canada's hybrid mismatch rules (Part XVI.1 of the Income Tax Act) and Quebec's corresponding rules override any arrangement designed to exploit differing entity classifications to produce double non-taxation; no elective pass-through regime beyond statutory partnership status is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14.975%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies (both GST/HST and QST). Non-residents supplying commercial services or tangible property in Canada may be required to register without regard to the threshold if they solicit business in Canada or are required to collect QST under Quebec's place-of-supply rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports are zero-rated under the GST/HST and QST where the goods are exported 'as soon after the supply as is reasonable in the circumstances' and documentary proof of export is retained. Services supplied to non-residents (e.g., on-site factory pre-shipment inspection and testing services supplied to a foreign buyer for goods destined outside Canada) are generally zero-rated unless the service is in respect of real property situated in Canada or tangible personal property situated in Canada that is not exported. For inspection/testing services performed in Quebec on goods to be exported by the non-resident recipient, zero-rating applies if the supply is made to a non-resident who is not registered and the goods are exported as part of the transaction; the place-of-supply rules for QST must be reviewed to confirm no Quebec consumption.

  • Canada Customs Export Declaration (B13A or paper/e-manifest)
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill
  • Bank SWIFT remittance confirmation or foreign exchange contract evidencing receipt of consideration

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate, or digital stamping exists under Canadian federal or Quebec law. Electronic invoicing is voluntary, but CRA and Revenu Québec require registrants to maintain complete electronic books and records. Large businesses, as defined by Revenu Québec, must file QST returns electronically; suppliers to the Quebec public sector may be contractually required to use the province's electronic invoicing systems. API-based real-time reporting is not mandatory for general VAT registrants.

CRA and Revenu Québec may disallow the 0% rate if contemporaneous documentation does not establish export within a reasonable time or the service is not truly supplied to a non-resident. A reassessment will apply GST/HST and QST at the full combined 14.975% rate, plus interest and penalties. False or omission in export documentation attracts civil penalties under s.285 of the Excise Tax Act (up to 50% of the tax sought) and comparable Quebec penalties; intentional fraud exposes partners and directors to unlimited joint liability and criminal prosecution. Canada has no statutory FX repatriation deadline, but prolonged failure to receive export proceeds may be scrutinized as evidence that the supply was not truly exported or was consumed in Canada.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible across both current and capital account transactions; Canada does not operate exchange controls or maintain a waiting/authorization regime for profit remittance. A Quebec limited partnership may repatriate or distribute profits through its principal bank without central-bank application, subject to tax compliance: non-resident limited partners must file Canadian returns for allocable business income and may be subject to Part XIII withholding on certain payments at a 25% statutory rate, reduced by applicable income tax treaty if the tax-residence certificate and NR301 form are delivered to the paying Canadian agent before settlement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, repatriation, or forced-conversion obligation applies to export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency, placed in a Canadian foreign-currency bank account, or converted freely. There is no statutory timeline; reporting is limited to balance-of-payments data and FINTRAC large-transaction reporting (CAD 10,000 or more) and any prescribed SWIFT-related KYC declarations required by the receiving Canadian bank.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada); there is no separate Foreign Exchange Control Administration, with prudential banking supervision carried out by OSFI and AML/ATF supervision by FINTRAC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Wise (formerly TransferWise), as a FINTRAC-registered money-services business rather than a Schedule I Canadian bank, is viable at a Medium level for low-value cross-border operational payments and treasury concentration, but at a Low level as a primary account, capital escrow provider, or documentary credit channel. Wise cannot offer CDIC deposit insurance, cannot hold client escrow under a canonical Canadian legal arrangement, does not issue commercial letters of credit, and may require closure if account activity resembles a statutory trust or broker/dealer function. A Quebec LP should therefore use Wise only for incidental multi-currency disbursements and must pair it with a tier-1 depositary bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital from non-resident limited partners should be wired to the Quebec LP's tier-1 Schedule I bank account using SWIFT MT103. The remitter's and beneficiary's full legal names and addresses must match the corporate registry; the remittance instruction in MT103 field 70 should clearly state 'Capital contribution under LP agreement dated [date]' and include the LP's Quebec NEQ number and the capital call reference. For CAD 10,000 or more, the receiving bank will complete a FINTRAC large-currency transaction report and may ask for the partnership resolution and source-of-funds statement. There is no central-bank exchange-control approval or mandatory purpose-code regime; the bank's internal 'Direct Investment / Capital Call' payment category should be selected, and if the capital is denominated in foreign currency, the FX conversion is executed at the bank's standard interbank-plus-spread rate.For international trade settlements, the primary rails are SWIFT MT103 for direct payments and SWIFT MT202/COVER for interbank layering when amounts exceed a domestic threshold. For documentary trade, a Commercial Letter of Credit issued by a tier-1 Canadian bank is the recommended instrument, presented through the beneficiary's confirming bank. USD settlements are cleared over CHIPS/Fedwire through correspondent banks; CAD settlements are cleared through Payments Canada's Lynx high-value system. The MT103 remittance should always include the invoice number, inspection reference, and a description of services to permit straight-through processing and to avoid sanctions false positives. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined general federal and Quebec corporate income tax rate (15% federal + 11.5% Quebec); the Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent and is not taxed at the entity level, but corporate and individual partners are taxed on their allocated shares.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory withholding under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act for dividends paid by a Canadian-resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder; this rate does not apply to distributions from an LP because they are not dividends. LP distributions are returns of capital or allocations of partnership income and are taxed under Part I or Part XIII depending on the character of the underlying income.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CAD 1,000,000 for triggering Form T106 (Information Return for Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents) in a fiscal year. Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation, including Local File and Master File, is expected where cross-border related-party transactions in goods, services, or intangibles exceed CAD 1,000,000; Country-by-Country reporting is mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated revenue equal to or greater than EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, economic substance is substantively embedded in the general anti-avoidance rule (ITA section 245), the Quebec general anti-avoidance rule (Taxation Act section 1079.1), and the partnership anti-avoidance rules. A limited partnership that functions as a shell, lacks real premises, employs no staff, or conducts no genuine inspection/testing activity will be disregarded or recharacterized. The CRA may attribute the partnership income to the real taxpayer under the 'substance over form' doctrine, impose gross negligence penalties, and pursue criminal tax evasion for sham arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Statements

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of 50% of the understated tax under subsection 163(2) of the Income Tax Act; criminal conviction under section 239 carries a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and imprisonment for up to 5 years. Revenu Québec enforces parallel penalties under the Taxation Act.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and the general partner’s directors and managers may be held jointly liable for the penalties if they directed, assented to, or acquiesced in the omission; criminal liability attaches personally and may result in travel bans, asset freezes, and cross-border extradition.
Transfer Pricing Violations

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment under subsection 247(2); a 10% penalty on the net adjustment under subsection 247(4); Form T106 late-filing penalties for each non-resident transaction exceeding CAD 1,000,000. Quebec levies an additional penalty under section 1079.1 of the Taxation Act when the federal TP adjustment arises from a Quebec taxpayer.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in the establishment of non-arm's-length fees or who fail to ensure documentation is prepared may be assessed personally for penalties; the burden of proof shifts to the taxpayer, and civil restitution may include foreign assets.
Shell Company / Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The general anti-avoidance rule under section 245 of the Income Tax Act denies the tax benefit; a false incentive may trigger a 50% gross negligence penalty; a promoter or participant may be subject to penalties under the tax shelter reporting rules and, where money laundering is involved, forfeiture under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act.

Director personal liability: Directors and promoters are personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties; the corporate veil is pierced where the LP is a mere facade; directors may face travel bans, injunctions, and criminal imprisonment for fraud under section 380 of the Criminal Code.
AML / FINTRAC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative monetary penalties under the PCMLTFA can reach CAD 1,000,000 for a corporation and CAD 500,000 for an individual; criminal prosecution for failure to implement a compliance program can result in imprisonment up to 5 years; the regulator may also suspend or restrict business operations.

Director personal liability: Officers and directors are personally liable if they knowingly failed to establish and enforce an AML compliance regime; the court may issue civil restitution orders and travel bans; where the assets were derived from criminal activity, directors face seizure and criminal confinement.
