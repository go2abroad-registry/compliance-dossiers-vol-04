Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (CRA / Agence du revenu du Canada), administered via the 'My Business Account' digital portal, Business Registration Online (BRO), and GST/HST NETFILE. Ontario corporate-registry functions (limited partnership declaration, extra-provincial registration) are handled by the Ontario Business Registry under ServiceOntario. Ontario participates in the federal HST framework, so no separate Ontario provincial tax registration is required for GST/HST.

E-government filing portal

(1) Register the Ontario limited partnership with the Ontario Business Registry by filing the Declaration of Limited Partnership (Form 3) and identifying the registered general partner and office in Ontario; a foreign LP must file an extra-provincial registration. (2) Apply for the CRA Business Number (BN) online through Business Registration Online (BRO), selecting program accounts appropriate to the SPAC activity: 'RP' (payroll), 'RT' (GST/HST), 'RM/RE' (import/export) as applicable. (3) Register the GST/HST account (RT) if the LP carries on business in Canada and ceases to be a small supplier (worldwide taxable supplies exceeding CAD 30,000), or voluntarily under ETA s. 240(2.1); a SPAC whose only supplies are exempt financial services must not register merely to claim input tax credits, but must evaluate whether it is a 'selected financial institution' subject to the special attribution method under ETA Part IX. (4) File Form RC445 or use BRO to evidence partnership registration particulars, appoint a Canadian representative if non-resident, and submit the partnership agreement, foreign certificate of good standing (for extra-provincial entities), and an authorization letter for CRA 'My Business Account' access. (5) Obtain or evidence Canadian tax identifiers for each non-resident partner: corporate partners may use a cross-referenced foreign corporate number or a CRA non-resident account; individual partners must apply for an Individual Tax Number (ITN) using Form T1261. (6) File T5013 and T5013 Summary information returns where the partnership carries on business in Canada or derives Canadian-source income allocable to partners, and remit Part X.2 tax on amounts allocable to non-resident partners. (7) For SPAC trust/escrow operations, the escrow trust itself is not separately GST/HST-registered; administrative and escrow fees from Ontario suppliers bear 13% HST, which is generally a stranded cost unless an ETA s. 273 related-party election or a financial-institution special attribution method is available.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — an Ontario limited partnership is fiscally transparent under ITA s. 96: income, losses, and credits are computed at the partnership level and flow through to partners in accordance with their partnership interests; the LP is not a Part I taxpayer in the ordinary case. Overrides and territorial nuances: (i) a publicly traded limited partnership may be classified as a SIFT partnership under ITA s. 122.1(1), becoming subject to entity-level Part I tax at the general corporate rate; (ii) non-resident partners are taxable only on their allocable share of Canadian-source income, generally attributed through the partnership's deemed permanent establishment in Canada; (iii) for GST/HST purposes, the LP is a separate registrant and is itself a financial institution when its principal business is investing in financial instruments, such as a SPAC holding trust assets.

Available election forms

Canada does not provide a U.S.-style 'check-the-box' classification election; statutory elections and deeming rules are limited but material: (a) partnership fiscal-period election under ITA s. 249.1(4) to adopt a non-calendar partnership year; (b) functional-currency election under ITA s. 261 for reporting in a partner's or the partnership's functional currency; (c) election under ITA s. 102(2) deeming each non-resident member to not be a member of the partnership, preserving 'Canadian partnership' treatment for certain provisions; (d) hybrid-mismatch ordering and election rules under ITA ss. 18.4–18.6 aligning Canada with the OECD BEPS Action 2 approach (including elections to treat certain deductible payments differently); (e) GST/HST close-connection election under ETA s. 273 to deem certain intercompany or partnership-related supplies to be made for nil consideration; and (f) voluntary GST/HST registration election under ETA s. 240(2.1) for non-residents carrying on business in Canada.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CAD 30,000 — the 'small supplier' threshold: a person, including a non-resident partnership carrying on business in Canada, becomes a mandatory GST/HST registrant once its worldwide taxable supplies (including those of associated persons) exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over the preceding four consecutive calendar quarters. Registration is voluntary below that threshold, subject to ETA conditions; a person making only exempt supplies (e.g., a pure SPAC LP whose sole activity is issuing units and holding escrowed securities) cannot recover input tax credits through ordinary GST/HST registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Tangible personal property qualifies for 0% GST/HST where it is exported from Canada in accordance with ETA Schedule VI Part V, with the supplier retaining contemporaneous transport and export evidence. Services are zero-rated under Schedule VI Part V section 7 when supplied to a non-resident person, unless the service relates to real property situated in Canada, tangible personal property situated in Canada at the time the service is performed, or is supplied to an individual present in Canada; off-shore services performed and delivered outside Canada also qualify where the non-resident test is met. Financial services, which constitute the central commercial activity of a SPAC, are default-exempt under Schedule V Part VII rather than zero-rated; only the narrow cross-border financial services enumerated in Schedule VI Part IX (e.g., certain fee-based services provided to non-residents outside Canada) can be zero-rated. Zero-rating therefore requires proof that the supply was made to an export or non-resident recipient, that the economic performance occurred outside Canada where applicable, that a commercial invoice was issued to the non-resident, and that payment traces to the export recipient.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed electronically with the Canada Border Services Agency through the Canadian Export Reporting System / Canada's Trade single-window (equivalent of CBSA Form B13A)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, or equivalent transport document evidencing physical departure of the goods from Canada
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, bank payment receipt, or foreign-exchange contract evidencing receipt and repatriation of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice and underlying supply or acquisition agreement expressly naming the non-resident recipient and specifying shipment/delivery terms
  • Non-resident status evidence for counterparties and partners, including CRA Form NR301 or certificates of foreign tax residence

Canada currently imposes no statutory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or mandatory digital stamping. The CRA operates a voluntary Pan-Canadian e-invoicing framework aligned with the Peppol network (PEPPOL BIS 3.0) for B2B/B2G exchange; participation is optional, while CRA encourages it as the future standard. Mandatory digital compliance already applies in part: registrants with annual taxable supplies exceeding CAD 1 million must file GST/HST returns electronically via GST/HST NETFILE, and payments above CAD 10 million must be remitted electronically. Books and records must be kept in an electronic or paper format acceptable to the Minister under ETA s. 286, and every invoice issued by a registrant must show the supplier's Business Number, the GST/HST registration number, and the amount of tax charged.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to CRA post-assessment review and income-tax-style documentation audits; failure to retain the complete statutory books, records, and underlying export evidence under ETA ss. 286–287 allows the Minister to deny the 0% rate retrospectively, reassess the supply at 13% HST (or 5% GST where applicable), claw back refunds with interest, and extend the normal assessment window under ETA s. 298 where misrepresentation is attributable to neglect, carelessness, wilful default, or fraud. Civil penalties for false statements in returns or refund claims may reach CAD 25,000 per occurrence under ETA s. 280; gross negligence or wilful participation in a false claim can trigger penalties of up to 50% of the tax sought to be evaded under ETA s. 285, and criminal tax-evasion convictions expose fines and imprisonment (summary conviction fines up to CAD 25,000 and/or imprisonment up to 12 months; indictment-level exposure up to CAD 250,000 and up to 5 years). CRA also profiles exporters and financial-holding vehicles by refund-to-assessed-liability ratios, making SPAC structures with zero-rated or exempt claims a heightened audit cohort.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility of the Canadian dollar (CAD) under the Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) and the repealed Foreign Exchange Control Act (suspended and effectively abolished in 1951). There is no prior-approval regime, no surrender duty, no CAD repatriation quota, and no restriction on profit remittance or dividend distribution to non-resident partners. The only functionally relevant hurdles are (i) Part XIII withholding tax on payments to non-residents under the Income Tax Act (Canada), generally 25% subject to treaty relief, with a reduced rate of 15% or less where an income tax treaty applies; (ii) the Investment Canada Act notification or review process where a non-Canadian establishes a new Canadian business or acquires control of a Canadian entity; and (iii) the CRA Form T5013 allocation and T106 reporting obligations for non-resident partners of a Canadian limited partnership. Capital flows into a SPAC trust escrow are fully convertible and freely transferable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio whatsoever for export proceeds. CAD is a freely floated currency and exporters and dealers are permitted to retain, hold, and disburse foreign currency balances without any surrender requirement, central bank approval, or liquidation obligation. The sole administrative duties are (i) FINTRAC reporting of the receipt of C$10,000 or more in international electronic funds transfers, and (ii) CRA reporting of foreign-source income and foreign property exceeding C$100,000 in cost amount (Form T1135). For a SPAC, this freedom means escrowed IPO/private-placement proceeds may be held in USD or CAD at the trustee without restriction.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada) is the monetary authority responsible for currency issuance and official international reserves management, while foreign-exchange regulation functions are exercised through the Department of Finance Canada, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) for prudential banking supervision, and the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) for transaction-level cross-border reporting. No centralized exchange-approval agency similar to an exchange-control ministry exists in Canada.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low legal viability for the SPAC trust/escrow function. Canadian federal and provincial statutes reserve deposit-taking and trust activities to entities licensed under the Bank Act (Canada) or the Ontario Loan and Trust Corporations Act; offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Multitude, Airwallex) are generally not authorized deposit-taking institutions in Canada and may not lawfully hold SPAC subscription proceeds, escrowed IPO funds, or partnership capital on a custodial basis. These platforms are permissible only as secondary settlement utilities for low-value operating expenses and client-disbursement processing, conditional on compliance with the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), which establishes a licensing and risk-management regime for payment service providers; the RPAA's transitional registration requirement took effect in 2024-2025 and cannot substitute for a Schedule I bank or provincial trust company as escrow agent.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound subscription capital from non-resident partners is routed by SWIFT MT103 (or Fedwire through the bank's New York correspondent where the remitting institution is USD-denominated) to the SPAC's Canadian dollar or USD operating and segregated trust accounts at the chosen Schedule I bank. Canada has no statutorily prescribed purpose-code registry; however, best practice requires the wire narrative to clearly state: SPAC Trust Escrow / Capital Contribution / Ontario Limited Partnership, the CRA business number and the Ontario registry identification number of the limited partnership. Supplementary compliance documentation must include (i) the executed subscription agreement; (ii) beneficial-ownership and source-of-wealth declarations for each remitter; (iii) W-8BEN-E or W-9 forms where US persons are involved; (iv) an Investment Canada Act notification (Form ISED-201) to Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada where a non-Canadian establishes the SPAC as a new Canadian business or acquires control of a Canadian business; and (v) a FINTRAC Large Virtual Currency Transaction Report or International Electronic Funds Transfer Report (automatically filed by the bank) for amounts at or above C$10,000. The escrow trustee must furthermore confirm that the funds have been released from general operating cash flow and ring-fenced in a segregated trust account pending the qualifying acquisition.Outbound settlement follows dual rails: (i) for high-value, time-critical acquisition payments, SWIFT MT103 priority transfers routed through the bank's Canadian Payments Canada (Settlement Optimizer / LVTS-equivalent) domestic clearing for CAD, and via the bank's US correspondent for USD, with a compliance requirement that all MT103 messages include the full legal name of the payee, their bank (SWIFT BIC), the reference to the business-combination agreement, and confirmation that the threshold for the Payment Clearing and Settlement Act, R.S.C. 1996, c. 6, s. 4 is applied; and (ii) for documentary trade obligations, the SPAC will issue commercial letters of credit governed by UCP600 and, where applicable, standby LCs (ISP98) through the Canadian bank's trade finance division, with defaults under the International Factoring and Receivables programs; the trustee requires two co-signatories, a settlement release certificate signed by the escrow agent, and a legal opinion confirming the target business combination has been consummated before any trust-to-operating transfer. Where the outbound transfer exceeds US$1 million, the bank will apply an enhanced fraud-prevention callback and the FINTRAC EFT reporting threshold will automatically trigger a Suspicious Transaction Report only if anomalous patterns are detected.Plain vanilla over-the-counter (OTC) foreign-exchange forwards in CAD/USD with fixed notional and maturity, documented under the ISDA 2002 Master Agreement and Credit Support Annex; such contracts are statutorily permissible without Bank of Canada or OSFI authorization because CAD is a fully convertible floating currency, and Canadian Schedule I banks act as counterparties for notional amounts up to internal counterparty credit limits.Cross-currency swaps (FX swaps and cross-currency interest rate swaps) between CAD and USD, including principal-exchange variants, booked under an ISDA Master Agreement with standardized CSA margin posting; permissible and commonly used to hedge USD-denominated SPAC trust corpus into CAD while overturning NAV volatility.European-style OTC FX options, participating forwards, and collars traded bilaterally with the Canadian bank dealing desk exclusively for genuine hedging of the SPAC's disclosed USD/CAD acquisition exposure; no restricted dynamic hedging seat or derivative authorization is required under Ontario securities law where the SPAC is a non-dealer end-user using derivatives for non-speculative treasury purposes.Exchange-traded CAD/USD futures and options on futures listed on CME Group (e.g., 6C contract) and ICE; permissible for the SPAC because exchange-traded derivatives are cleared through the Canadian Derivatives Clearing Corporation (CDCC) for domestic execution, and the financial institution will accept them only where the treasury board minutes document the hedge objective.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in CAD are technically permissible but operationally marginal because the Bank of Canada publishes a well-established WM/Refinitiv (now LSEG) fixing, meaning deliverable forwards are normally cheaper and more liquid; use of an NDF would be accepted solely where the SPAC needs offshore USD liquidity without CAD physical settlement, and requires an explicit board-approved rationale, ISDA 2021 definitions, and confirmation that no underlying speculative intent exists.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at the Ontario limited partnership entity level; the LP is fiscally transparent under Part I of the Income Tax Act (Canada) (ITA) and income is allocated to partners. Applicable headline rates at partner level: combined federal/Ontario general corporate rate 26.5% (federal 15% + Ontario 11.5%) for Canadian-resident corporate general partners; combined top marginal rate up to 53.53% for Ontario-resident individual partners; non-resident corporate partners — federal Part I rate 15% on allocated Canadian business income plus 25% branch tax under Part XIV (treaty-reduced to 15% or 5% under the Canada-US DTA) unless exempt; non-resident individual partners — progressive Part I rates on allocated Canadian business income, without the spouse credit, plus Part XIII withholding on non-business passive income allocations.
