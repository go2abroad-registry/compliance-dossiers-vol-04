Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Canada Revenue Agency (Agence du revenu du Canada). Federal portal: CRA My Business Account via https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html. Ontario corporate income tax is administered by the Canada Revenue Agency on behalf of the Ontario Ministry of Finance under the Tax Collection Agreement; provincial business registry is the Ontario Business Registry (https://www.ontario.ca/page/ontario-business-registry).

E-government filing portal

A corporation obtains a 9-digit Business Number (BN) from the CRA through Business Registration Online or by filing Form RC1. After Ontario incorporation or extra-provincial registration, the entity must add program accounts as applicable: RC for corporate income tax, RT for GST/HST, RP for payroll, and RM for import/export reporting. GST/HST registration becomes mandatory once worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated supplies and excluding exempt supplies, exceed CAD 30,000 in any four consecutive calendar quarters; a non-resident corporation may also be required to register if it carries on business in Canada and makes taxable supplies. The TIN for Canadian tax purposes is the BN, and all returns are filed under the same BN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A business corporation is a separate taxable person: resident corporations pay federal Part I income tax and applicable Ontario corporate tax on worldwide income; non-resident corporations are taxable in Canada only on Canadian-source business income attributable to a permanent establishment and on certain Canadian-source investment income. There is no pass-through to shareholders.

Available election forms

Canada has no US-style check-the-box election; entity classification is statutory under the Income Tax Act and the Excise Tax Act. Relevant elections include: (i) ETA section 156 election between closely related corporations to treat intercorporate supplies as consideration-nil for GST/HST purposes; (ii) ETA section 167 election for a supply of a business as a going concern; (iii) ITA section 216(1) election for a non-resident corporation to be taxed on a net basis on Canadian-source business or property income; (iv) ITA section 216(4) election for certain non-resident rental and real property income; and (v) anti-hybrid mismatch rules which are non-elective, but tax-deductibility outcomes must be mapped before any hybrid financing or foreign affiliate structure is implemented.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GST/HST registration threshold: CAD 30,000 in worldwide taxable supplies over any four consecutive calendar quarters. Zero-rated supplies count as taxable supplies; exempt financial supplies do not. A non-resident virtual card issuer must also test whether its supplies of card issuing, account management, and payment processing services are made in Canada under the place-of-supply rules and whether the entity is carrying on business in Canada so as to require GST/HST registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated HST treatment applies when goods are exported from Canada as qualifying supplies under Schedule VI, Part V of the ETA, or when services are supplied to a non-resident for use primarily outside Canada and are not related to Canadian real property or tangible personal property located in Canada. For a corporate virtual card issuer, core card issuance and transaction-clearing fees may be exempt financial services rather than zero-rated services, so a detailed supply characterization opinion is required. A GST/HST registrant reports zero-rated revenues in the return to obtain net tax refunds, but exempt financial supplies must be segregated from any zero-rating claim.

  • CBSA export reporting record, including Canadian Export Reporting System (CERS) declaration or Form A1 for controlled or regulated goods
  • Commercial invoice and purchase agreement showing the non-resident recipient and sale terms
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing actual shipment from Canada
  • Carrier certificate, border release, or destination customs receipt proving physical export
  • Bank or foreign exchange remittance confirmation / inward remittance certificate evidencing payment from the non-resident buyer

Canada has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing, or digital stamping regime for private sector B2B or B2C transactions. Suppliers must issue invoices containing the prescribed GST/HST information under ETA sections 221-223 and applicable CRA guidance; electronic invoices are permissible if legible and reproducible on audit. GST/HST returns are filed electronically through CRA My Business Account, GST/HST NETFILE, or certified service providers. Books, records, invoices, and export substantiation must be retained for six years.

The CRA may reverse a zero-rating claim where the exporter does not maintain contemporaneous documentary proof of export and payment. If an export supply is reclassified as taxable, the supplier must remit 13% HST with arrears interest. Civil penalties for false or misleading statements apply under ETA section 327, generally the greater of CAD 100 and 25% of the amount claimed, increasing to 50% for wilful misrepresentation, and criminal prosecution may follow under sections 380 or 400 of the Criminal Code. The normal four-year reassessment period does not apply where misrepresentation, neglect, or fraud is present, and CRA may retrospectively reassess, claw back refunds, and charge penalties without a limitations bar.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Canadian dollar is fully convertible. Canada maintains no general exchange control regime; capital account and current account transactions are free for non-residents. Profit remittances such as dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees may be freely repatriated after compliance with Canadian withholding taxes and treaty documentation. For a regulated virtual card issuer, no FX permit is required for capital injection or trade payments, but all settlement balances must be held through a Canadian regulated financial institution or via licensed MSB channels.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender rules exist. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency, converted at will, or remitted to Canada without timeline or conversion ratio requirements. FINTRAC reporting and sanctions screening obligations still apply to large cross-border EFTs.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada and the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI); foreign exchange administrative oversight also involves the Department of Finance Canada and FINTRAC for cross-border transaction monitoring.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

26.5% combined (federal 15% + Ontario provincial 11.5%) for a general business corporation; a fintech corporate virtual-card issuer that is a specified financial institution is ineligible for the Small Business Deduction and is taxed at the full general rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Part XIII gross withholding tax on dividends paid or credited to non-residents under ITA s. 212(2); capital dividends and stock dividends carry separate Part XIII treatment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation (Local File and Master File) is mandatory in any taxation year in which the taxpayer and its related parties have combined gross revenues exceeding CAD 1,000,000 and the taxpayer enters into reportable transactions with non-arm's-length non-residents. Country-by-Country Reporting applies to MNE groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least CAD 1,000,000,000 (the adopted OECD EUR 750M-equivalent threshold).

Economic substance scope: No dedicated Economic Substance Regulations exist in Canada, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (s. 245 ITA) recharacterizes arrangements lacking genuine economic substance, and the OECD Multilateral Instrument's Principal Purpose Test (PPT) restricts treaty benefits for shell structures. The Ontario virtual-card issuer must maintain real mind-and-management, Canadian personnel, risk and compliance functions, and operational substance; CRA may reallocate income under s. 247(2) when the Canadian entity lacks the capacity to earn reported card-related returns.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Wilful Blindness in Corporate Filings

Statutory sanction: ITA s. 239(1): on summary conviction, a fine of 50% to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded and/or imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment, a fine of 100% to 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; additionally, a civil gross-negligence penalty of 50% of understated tax (s. 163(2)) and compounded interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who direct, authorize, or acquiesce in false filings are chargeable as parties; personal fines, restitution orders, court-ordered travel bans, and imprisonment up to 5 years; CRA may also assess directors for unremitted withholding liabilities under s. 227.1 ITA without proof of intent.
Unlicensed Card Issuance / Shell Corporation Operation

Statutory sanction: RPAA s. 87: for a corporation, on summary conviction a fine up to CAD 5,000,000 and on indictment a fine up to CAD 25,000,000, plus compliance orders, suspension or revocation of registration; the Bank of Canada may also impose administrative monetary penalties for non-compliance with risk-management duties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly permit unregistered activity are personally liable to prosecution; on indictment, imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to CAD 5,000,000; courts may impose travel restrictions as bail or sentencing conditions and order forfeiture of corporate proceeds.
