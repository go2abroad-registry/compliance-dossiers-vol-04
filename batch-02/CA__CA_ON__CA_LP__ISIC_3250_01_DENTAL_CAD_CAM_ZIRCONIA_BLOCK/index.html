Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Canada operates a free-floating, fully convertible currency with no exchange controls on either the current account or the capital account. The Currency Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. C-52) designates CAD as legal tender, but Canadian residents and non-residents may freely contract, invoice, and settle in USD, EUR, CNY, or any other currency. Current-account flows - payments for imported zirconia powder, milling machinery, royalties, freight, commissions, and technical services - require no ministerial authorization or permit. Capital-account flows - capital subscriptions into the Ontario Limited Partnership (LP), intercompany shareholder loans, profit distributions, and loan repayments - are equally unrestricted in form. Practical frictions are tax and banking frictions rather than FX-control frictions: (i) a non-Canadian sponsor establishing a new Canadian business must file a notification under the Investment Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. 28 (1st Supp.)) within 30 days; dental ceramic block manufacturing is not a reviewable cultural business; (ii) the Income Tax Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.)) Part XIII and subsection 153(1.1) impose withholding on distributions and allocations to non-resident partners, typically 15% under the Canada-United States Income Tax Convention and subject to the LP's T5013 allocation mechanics; (iii) FINTRAC reporting obligations apply to financial institutions for electronic fund transfers of CAD 10,000 or more; the client itself files no FX report; and (iv) OSFI-regulated banks retain internal enhanced due diligence policies for non-resident-controlled entities. Profit remittance is therefore legally immediate and unblocked; it is a taxable event, not a regulated conversion event.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Canada imposes no statutory timeline for repatriation of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender of export receipts into the domestic banking system. The Ontario LP may retain 100% of foreign-currency receipts in segregated USD or EUR accounts at domestic banks (e.g., RBC USD Chequing Account, TD USD Daily Interest Account), may net them against import payables, or may hold them offshore, subject to Income Tax Act information reporting (notably Form T1135 for specified foreign property and, where applicable, Form T1134 for controlled foreign affiliates; status to be confirmed with tax counsel because the LP itself is generally resident in Canada for Part I purposes when it carries on business in Canada). The only operational export obligation is documentary: under the Customs Act and the Export Control List, an export declaration is required through the CBSA Canadian Export Reporting System (CAERS) for goods exported from Canada valued at CAD 2,000 or more, or for controlled goods; non-compliance attracts penalties but has no foreign-exchange consequence.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Canada (Banque du Canada), established under the Bank of Canada Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. B-2), is the central monetary authority and statutory supervisor of designated payment systems operated by Payments Canada (formerly the Canadian Payments Association under the Canadian Payments Act, R.S.C., 1985, c. C-21). The foreign-exchange administrative function is distributed: the Department of Finance Canada holds exchange-rate policy; the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) prudentially supervises banks under the Bank Act (S.C. 1991, c. 46); FINTRAC administers the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (S.C. 2000, c. 17) and its Regulations (SOR/2002-184). There is no dedicated exchange-control authority, no FX approval desk, and no requirement to obtain central-bank clearance for any commercial payment, capital contribution, or repatriation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: B- (Medium-Low) as the primary operational banking rail, but High as a supplementary collection and payables rail. Offshore non-bank electronic money institutions (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer) can open multi-currency CAD/USD/EUR wallets for the Ontario LP and provide SWIFT-grade routing, but: (i) they are not banks under the federal Bank Act and do not settle directly at the Bank of Canada, so all CAD clearing ultimately settles through a domestic Schedule I bank; (ii) effective September 8, 2025, the Retail Payment Activities Act (S.C. 2021, c. 23) and the Retail Payment Activities Regulations (SOR/2023-229) require any payment service provider performing payment functions in Canada to be registered with the Bank of Canada; an unregistered offshore EMI would be non-compliant and creates legal and reputational risk for the LP as a user; (iii) Canadian-origin treasury functions required by an operating manufacturer - CRA payroll remittance, cheque imaging, Lynx same-day CAD settlement, trade finance (commercial letters of credit and banker's acceptances), USD clearing, and Health Canada/CBSA documentation flows - are not fully substitutable by EMIs; (iv) EMIs enforce FINTRAC-grade KYC on the LP but often apply lower transaction ceilings and higher friction for high-value dental trade settlements; (v) recommended architecture: maintain the Schedule I bank account as the sole treasury hub, and use an EMI only for low-value recurring collections from US and EU dental distributor customers.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Recommended rail: a single-leg SWIFT MT103 sent from the foreign sponsor's own bank account (never from a third party) to the LP's CAD operating account at the Canadian Schedule I bank. The message must carry the referencing line: 'capital contribution - Ontario LP, Limited Partnership Agreement dated [date], Schedule A capital call, ISIC 3250 dental block manufacturing'. If the sponsor's funding currency is USD, the bank should credit a dedicated USD sub-account to avoid double conversion; CAD conversion is executed at the same bank during the Ontario FX trading window (approximately 09:15-17:00 ET). Sequence of pre-funding steps: (i) complete Ontario LP registration and obtain the Limited Partnership Declaration; (ii) file the Investment Canada Act new-business notification with ISED and retain the acknowledgment; (iii) complete all bank CDD and sign the account mandate; (iv) confirm account operational status in writing before transmission; (v) transmit one clean wire and retain the SWIFT confirmation, bank statement, and capital-call voucher. Post-receipt expectations: the bank files a FINTRAC EFT report for any transfer of CAD 10,000 or more; transfers above CAD 250,000 may be pended for 2-5 business days for sanctions screening and correspondent review; the LP should proactively deliver the source-of-funds package listed above to shorten any hold. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined federal (15% for manufacturing/processing general rate, 28.0% federal general) and Ontario provincial (11.5% for manufacturing) effective combined statutory rate typically ranges from 26.5% to 28.5%; for M&P eligible income, combined rate approx. 25.5% (federal 15% + Ontario 10.5% M&P rate; note Ontario's general rate is 11.5%, M&P preferential rate 10.0% as of 2025). Exact determination depends on provincial allocation and M&P eligibility under subsection 125.1(1) ITA.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

For a Limited Partnership, distributions are not dividends; profit allocations to non-resident partners are generally subject to Canadian non-resident withholding tax under Part XIII ITA (25% statutory rate) unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty. However, actively carried-on business income allocated to a non-resident partner of a partnership may be exempt from Part XIII withholding if the income is attributable to the partner's permanent establishment in Canada and the partner files a T2 return (for corporations) or a T1 return (for individuals) disclosing the income; otherwise a 25% Part XIII withholding applies on the non-business allocation.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a Limited Partnership with fiscal year-end December 31, the T5013 return must be filed no later than March 31 of the following calendar year (i.e., more than 6 months after fiscal year end; specifically, the due date is 5 months after the fiscal period end or March 31, whichever is earlier, for partnerships with no participation in T2 corporations). If the LP has corporate partners, the T5013 due date is aligned with the partner's T2 deadline, generally 6 months after the fiscal year end.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Corporate partner's T2 due 2 months after its own taxation year-end (or 3 months for CCPCs claiming the small business deduction); for 2025 taxation years, the standard deadline remains 6 months after the year-end for the return submission, with the payment due 2 months after year-end (or 3 months for CCPC balance due).

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due 1 month after the fiscal year-end for annual filers; for quarterly filers, 1 month after the end of each fiscal quarter.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under subsection 247(4) ITA, CRA requires contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation (Local File) where the total value of cross-border transactions with non-resident related parties exceeds C$1,000,000 (or C$50,000 for loans and interest revenue/expense). Master File is required for entities with total annual revenue exceeding C$50 million and intra-group transactions exceeding C$5 million, or assets exceeding C$50 million. Canada has adopted the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and CRA Information Circular IC 87-2R, IC 94-4R; the CRA may impose a penalty under s.247(3) of 10% of the net transfer-pricing adjustment (minimum C$5,000) when documentation is not provided and the adjustment exceeds the threshold.

Economic substance scope: Canada does not apply the OECD 'substantial activity' ESR directly to Ontario corporations, but the Ontario Limited Partnership must demonstrate real economic substance (personnel, premises, banking discretion, decision-making in Canada) to avoid GAAR (General Anti-Avoidance Rule, s.245 ITA) and to sustain treaty benefits under the OECD Multilateral Instrument (MLI) – including the Principal Purpose Test (PPT). Having no local employees, management, or bank accounts would make the vehicle a 'shell' and could result in the CRA re-characterizing profit allocations under s.17(1) or treaty shopping under the MLI; to that end, the entity must have a permanent establishment in Ontario and employ at least one qualified manager or engineer resident in Canada, an operational lease or owned facility, and independent valuation evidence for intellectual property ownership.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Failure to Report Canadian-Source Partnership Income

Statutory sanction: Under subsection 239(1) ITA, every person who knowingly or under circumstances of negligence makes a false statement in a return is liable on summary conviction to a fine of not less than 50% nor more than 200% of the amount of tax sought to be evaded plus imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment, a fine up to 5 times the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years. CRA may also levy gross-negligence penalty under s.163(2) equal to 50% of the tax understated, plus interest with an allocated portion of the penalty.

Director personal liability: Under subsection 227.1(1) ITA, directors of a corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's and partnership's source-deduction obligations (payroll, GST/HST) if they fail to exercise the standard of care expected. Additionally, under s.239(1.1), a court may order an individual (including a limited partner who participated in management) to pay restitution of the evaded tax plus penalty; a conviction can impose a travel ban under the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act s.36 (inadmissibility due to serious criminality), and under the Criminal Code s.462.33, proceeds of crime may be subject to forfeiture. The CRA's criminal investigations unit may refer the case to the Public Prosecution Service of Canada; sentencing can include up to 5 years imprisonment for a tax evasion indictment. Consequently, an individual cannot cross the U.S.-Canada border or obtain a visa without disclosing the conviction.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the GAAR (s.245), the CRA may disallow the tax benefit, re-assess the income in the hands of the partners, and apply the 25% Part XIII withholding on distributions; additionally, the Canada Revenue Agency can levy a penalty under s.163(2) of 50% of the tax understated for gross negligence in maintaining inadequate books. The Ontario Ministry of Finance may also cancel the provincial registration for non-compliance with the Business Names Act and the Limited Partnerships Act (Ontario), resulting in revocation of the limited partner's limited-liability shield.

Director personal liability: If the corporate general partner is insolvent and has no real capital, limited partners who participate in control of the partnership business lose limited liability protection under Ontario's Limited Partnerships Act (O Reg 1182/19, ss.6-9), making all partners jointly and severally liable for partnership debts; individual general partners face personal liability for all partnership obligations, including tax debts and trade payables. Non-compliance with substance requirements may also cause a foreign court to pierce the corporate veil; in such case, the directors and controlling limited partners may be liable in tort for fraudulent misrepresentation or civil conspiracy; under Ontario's Business Corporations Act s.130, directors are personally accountable for wages not paid (up to 6 months) and up to 12 months of vacation pay.
Transfer Pricing – Unsupported Intercompany Charges

Statutory sanction: Under s.247(2), the CRA may adjust the transaction to an arm's-length amount and levy a penalty of 10% of the net adjustment, minimum C$5,000, if contemporaneous records are absent; this penalty is in addition to reassessed income tax and interest (prescribed rate 7% compounded daily); repeated gross negligence triggers the 50% gross-negligence penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner who knowingly signed transfer-pricing documentation they knew to be false could face criminal liability for tax evasion under s.239(1) (upon indictment, 5 years imprisonment and fines); civilly, they are jointly and severally liable for the tax debt of the corporation under subsections 227.1(1) and 240(1) when the source deductions remain unpaid; also, professional directors may be sued by the creditor or partners for misfeasance in office to recover misappropriated corporate funds under Ontario common law.
Anti-Money-Laundering (AML) Red Flag – Cash-Intensive Dental Distribution / Wire Structuring

Statutory sanction: Under PCMLTFA s.74, the failure to report a large virtual currency or cash transaction above C$10,000 carries an administrative monetary penalty (AMP) of up to C$100,000 for an individual and C$500,000 for an entity; structured transactions are a criminal offence (s.107.1 PCMLTFA) punishable by up to 5 years imprisonment; FINTRAC can also suspend or revoke the entity's MSB registration and issue compliance orders.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally named in FINTRAC compliance orders and monetary penalties if they were responsible for the reporting failure or knowingly permitted CDD (Customer Due Diligence) violations; under s.83.01 of the Criminal Code, a director who directs or assists in a crime becomes a party to the offence and is liable to the same punishment as the principal, including imprisonment; a criminal AML conviction triggers a travel ban under IRPA s.36(1)(b), exclusion from the U.S. Visa Waiver Program, and seizure of personal assets under s.462.37.
Failure to Register for Ontario Health Care Levy / Payroll Tax Compliance

Statutory sanction: Ontario Ministry of Finance imposes a 10% late-filing penalty of the amount of EHT due, plus interest at the prescribed provincial rate (currently 13% annually, compounded) from the date the return was due; failure to remit payroll source deductions triggers director liability under CRA s.227.1.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the unremitted employee withholdings (income tax, CPP, EI) up to C$50,000 per director if the corporation fails, plus interest; the CRA's director liability provisions (s.227.1(2)) require a due-diligence defence, but a failure to supervise payroll personnel is fatal; Ontario's EHT regulations similarly allow assessment against a director or officer for the unremitted EHT if the entity is dissolved or bankrupt.
